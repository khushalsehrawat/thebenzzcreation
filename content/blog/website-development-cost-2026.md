## Start with the business task

Explain what the website should help a visitor do. A service website may need to explain an offer and collect a qualified enquiry. A customer portal may need accounts, permissions and access to existing records. Both can contain a similar number of screens while requiring very different engineering and operational work.

Describe a complete journey, including what happens after the main action. An enquiry form needs validation, a reliable destination, confirmation and failure handling. A booking flow needs availability, cancellation rules and an owner for exceptions. Those details make an estimate useful. A request for a professional website leaves too many responsibilities open to interpretation.

## Separate the main cost drivers

| Cost driver | Questions to answer | Why it affects scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content | Who writes, supplies and approves it? | Preparation and revision need time |
| Functionality | Which complete tasks must work? | Interfaces require rules and testing |
| Integration | What access and documentation exist? | Unknown dependencies need investigation |
| Operation | Who maintains accounts and services? | Launch creates continuing responsibilities |

Page count still matters, particularly when pages have different structures or substantial content. It is simply an incomplete measure by itself. Reusing a well-designed template differs from designing several unique interactive journeys. Ask which page types are included and which assumptions depend on the business supplying approved material.

## Account for content and information architecture

Inventory existing pages, images, downloads and useful URLs. Decide what should be retained, revised or removed. Identify who writes new copy and who approves claims, policies and customer evidence. Missing content can interrupt design and development, so it belongs in the delivery plan rather than an informal promise to provide it later.

Information architecture covers how visitors find and understand that material. A service description, pricing explanation and contact path need to support a coherent decision. Include navigation, internal links and content hierarchy in the scope. If the business needs multiple languages, treat translation and language-specific review as real work instead of assuming a language switch creates a finished version.

## Choose technology around editing and workflow

A content-led business site, connected web application and commerce store have different needs. Compare the editorial workflow, integrations, hosting and maintenance responsibilities before choosing a platform. A custom frontend may still need a CMS for staff to publish comfortably. A standard CMS may need careful extension review when business rules become complex.

Ask the team to explain how the proposed approach fits your own operation. Test an ordinary publishing or administration task rather than relying on a technology label. The [platform comparison guide](/blog/wordpress-vs-nextjs-vs-shopify) provides questions for evaluating those choices. A familiar platform can be useful, but familiarity alone does not establish that every required workflow is supported.

## Investigate integrations before fixing the estimate

List the CRM, booking system, payment provider or inventory tool that must connect to the website. Share public documentation and identify who can authorise access. Confirm that the required API operations exist on the relevant account plan. An application displaying data does not necessarily provide a supported way to update that data externally.

Test the hardest connection early where uncertainty is material. Include authentication, permissions, rate limits, duplicate events and failure handling. A short investigation may lead to a clearer proposal or reveal that the planned workflow needs adjustment. Record unresolved dependencies plainly so a provisional estimate is not mistaken for a commitment based on verified access.

## Include migration and URL preservation

Replacing a website involves more than building new pages. Existing content, search landing pages, files and links may need a migration plan. Inventory important URLs and map necessary changes to relevant destinations. Redirecting everything to the homepage can make the new site less useful to people arriving from an old link.

Confirm whether records, media and metadata can be exported from the current system. Review the quality of the source material and any manual reconciliation needed. Keep a rollback or recovery plan appropriate to the launch. Migration effort should be visible in the quote, especially when the supplier cannot inspect the old platform before estimating.

## Define testing and launch readiness

Agree checks for mobile layouts, keyboard interaction, form labels, validation and relevant browser behaviour. Connected systems need end-to-end tests that establish the intended record or message reaches its destination. Technical SEO checks should include rendered metadata, canonical URLs, crawlability and sitemap coverage. These are deliverables that can be reviewed, rather than general claims about quality.

Confirm domain, DNS, hosting and provider access before selecting a launch date. Identify who approves the release and who can reverse a deployment if a critical issue appears. External provider activation and delayed content approval can affect timing. A useful schedule names those dependencies instead of presenting a universal website delivery duration.

## Compare recurring costs and engagement models

Separate one-time work from hosting, subscriptions, domain renewals, payment charges and maintenance. Confirm the account owner and billing party for each provider. A low initial total can exclude a necessary service, while a broad package may include work the business does not need. Compare the same operating model before interpreting a price difference.

A defined project can fit understood requirements. Phased work can fit a product that needs a focused first release and later learning. Ongoing support can address an already operational system. The appropriate model depends on uncertainty and responsibilities. Our [pricing page](/pricing) explains the estimating process without publishing rates that the owner has not approved.

## Prepare a comparable request for proposals

1. Describe the audience and complete primary journey.
2. List required page types and content responsibilities.
3. Identify integrations, migration and access dependencies.
4. Define acceptance, handover and operational responsibilities.
5. Separate launch essentials from later improvements.
6. Ask for assumptions, exclusions and recurring charges.

Use the same brief when comparing proposals. Request clarification where a total does not explain what is included. Avoid turning a preferred budget into an unsupported claim about market pricing. Bring the brief to the [website development service](/services/website-development) for discovery, and retain the agreed scope as the reference for future changes. A useful quote makes the relationship between the work, dependencies and price understandable.
