## Begin with the job, not a technology preference

<p>Write three non-negotiable tasks, who performs them and how often. A marketing team publishing weekly needs a dependable editor and approval process. A merchant needs accurate products, orders and refunds. A business application may need unique user permissions and multi-step transactions.</p><p>Next, name the people who will run the result. A system that needs a developer for each ordinary change may be unsuitable for a small editorial team. Conversely, a familiar editor does not solve a complicated approval workflow merely because a plugin exists.</p><p>A fair comparison includes implementation and ongoing work. Do not assume a bespoke application will be faster, or that a managed platform will fit every business rule. Build a small proof of the hardest task before committing.</p>

## Setup effort and total development costs

<p>WordPress may shorten setup when an appropriate theme and editor fit the brief. Budget for content structure, configuration, training and safe updates. A custom theme or substantial integration can make it a significant engineering project.</p><p>Shopify can reduce the amount of commerce infrastructure a team must assemble. Confirm theme changes, app subscriptions, product imports and fulfilment work before comparing totals. Check <a href="https://www.shopify.com/pricing">official subscription terms</a> separately from implementation quotes.</p><p>Next.js starts with an application foundation rather than a complete editing or store administration system. Estimate CMS integration, authentication, operations and handover where needed. See the <a href="/blog/website-development-cost-2026">website cost planning model</a> for a scope-based comparison instead of assuming a platform determines the budget.</p>

## Performance and SEO need execution on every platform

<p>Each option can serve useful indexable pages. The delivered result depends on content, rendering choices, image sizing, third-party scripts and implementation. A heavy theme can undermine a CMS site; a poorly built custom frontend can do the same.</p><p>Before signing off, check one canonical URL per page, readable server-delivered content, metadata, crawl permissions, redirects for moved pages and a working sitemap. Measure representative mobile journeys. A framework logo is not evidence of good Core Web Vitals or improved search rankings.</p><p>WordPress offers publishing features and an extension ecosystem, as described in its <a href="https://wordpress.org/about/features/">official feature overview</a>. Next.js offers several <a href="https://nextjs.org/docs/app/getting-started/deploying">deployment approaches</a>; static export has feature limitations. Neither fact replaces project-level testing.</p>

## Security, scaling and maintenance

<p>List the systems that store customer information and who patches each one. WordPress ownership includes plugin and theme selection, backups and updates. The <a href="https://developer.wordpress.org/advanced-administration/security/hardening/">WordPress hardening guidance</a> is a practical starting point, not proof that a particular site is secure.</p><p>With a custom Next.js application, the engineering team owns application vulnerabilities, dependencies, permissions and operational failure handling. A managed store provider operates much of the platform, while you still control staff access, apps, configuration and the way customer information is used.</p><p>Scaling should begin with the actual pressure: many product variants, busy checkout periods, a large publishing archive or expensive personalized requests. Test that workload. Adding custom infrastructure to solve an imagined future traffic problem may make today's business harder to operate.</p>

## Recommendations for real business situations

<ul><li>Content-led professional firm: trial WordPress editing with the people who will publish. A small static site can also work when updates are rare.</li><li>Retail startup with standard products and shipping: trial a hosted Shopify store before commissioning custom commerce.</li><li>Customer portal with unique permissions and integrations: investigate a custom application, potentially using Next.js with existing business services.</li><li>Store with a distinctive editorial frontend: compare a conventional store theme against a separate frontend, including preview, search and cart testing.</li><li>Team with no ongoing engineering capacity: prioritize maintainable managed workflows and a clear support arrangement.</li></ul><p>These are fit recommendations, not guarantees. A merchant's eligibility for payment services, an editor's approval requirements or an integration's API limits can change the recommendation. Document these constraints before choosing a platform.</p>

## A decision checklist you can use in a proposal

<p>Ask each supplier to demonstrate the hardest ordinary task in the proposed system: publishing a page, refunding part of an order or changing a customer's access. Request an account and data handover plan. Check whether important exports remain usable outside the product.</p><p>Compare twelve-month and three-year operating scenarios, including subscriptions, apps, hosting, maintenance, migration and staff time. State what is excluded. Review what happens if you change supplier, add a language or need a new payment workflow.</p><p>Finally, write an acceptance checklist shared by the business and developer. Define editing, ordering, permissions, recovery and mobile checks in observable terms. If the team cannot explain who will operate the site after launch, the platform decision is unfinished.</p>

## Write an ownership decision before choosing the platform

<p>Describe who publishes content, reviews updates and operates the site after handover. Test that person's ordinary task in the proposed administration workflow, including image descriptions, metadata and previewing a change. Confirm account recovery and exports before accepting a dependency. A platform that looks suitable in a developer demonstration may still be impractical for the team responsible for keeping the website useful. Discuss the complete workflow with our <a href="/services/website-development">website development service</a>, then record the reasons for the choice alongside the assumptions that could change it.</p>
